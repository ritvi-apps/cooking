Origin

A wild fruit from the western foothills of South America, domesticated by the Aztecs, then feared in Europe as a poisonous nightshade until the 1800s. Its story is why the botanists still call it a fruit and the cooks still call it a vegetable.

Family
Solanaceae
Nightshade. Cousins: potato, eggplant, chili, tobacco.
Native to
Andean coast
Wild in Peru & Ecuador. Domesticated in Mesoamerica ~500 BC.
To Europe
1540s
Spanish returned with seeds. Treated as an ornamental for 200 years.
Botanically
A berry
Legally ruled a vegetable in the US in 1893 (for tariff purposes).
Why Europeans thought it was poisonous
Aristocrats ate off pewter plates. Tomato acid leached lead from the pewter — people got sick, blamed the fruit. Peasants used wooden plates and ate them safely for centuries.

Grow

An annual almost everywhere. Loves heat, hates cold roots and wet leaves. If you can give it 6 hours of sun and a deep drink twice a week, it will feed you.

Soil temp
> 15 °C
Transplant only after last frost — chilled roots stunt for the whole season.
pH
6.0 – 6.8
Slightly acidic. Add compost, avoid fresh manure (too much nitrogen → leaves, no fruit).
Sun
6+ hrs / day
South-facing wall in cooler climates. Shade cloth if > 32 °C.
Seed to fruit
60 – 100 days
Start seeds indoors 6–8 weeks before last frost.

Companion planting

🌿
Basil
Repels aphids & whitefly. Old growers claim it sweetens the fruit; science calls it inconclusive but tradition holds.
🥕
Carrots
Loosen soil around tomato roots. Plant early — carrots finish before tomatoes need the space.
🌼
Marigold
Root secretions suppress nematodes. Bright blooms confuse pest scouts.
🥔
Potato
Same family, same blights. One spreads disease to the other. Also compete for nutrients.
🥬
Cabbage / brassicas
Heavy feeders that starve the tomato. Also chemical antagonism at the root zone.
🌱
Fennel
Allelopathic — releases compounds that inhibit almost every neighbour. Grow alone or with dill only.

Common varieties

🍅
Roma / Plum
Meaty, low water, few seeds. The default for sauces & paste.
🍒
Cherry
Highest sugar, thin skin. Best raw — bursts in salad, blisters in pan.
🍅
Beefsteak
Big slices for burgers and sandwiches. Balanced sweet-acid, high water.
🍅
San Marzano
The pizza tomato. Long, dense, low seed count. The DOP ones grow near Vesuvius.
🟢
Green Zebra
Ripe when yellow-green striped. Bright, tart. Great with feta.
🍅
Heirloom (Cherokee, Brandywine)
Genetic diversity = deeper flavor, thinner skin, shorter shelf life. Eat within 2 days.

How to pick

Signs it's good
Heavy for its size. Deep, even color to the shoulder. Slight give under thumb — not squishy. Sweet, grassy smell at the stem end (this is the #1 test).
Walk away if
Green shoulders (picked unripe). Soft or split skin. Refrigerated at the store (mealy inside). Smells like nothing at the stem.
On the Dirty Dozen — buy organic if you can
Tomato skin holds pesticide residue. EWG tests find 4+ pesticides on 92% of conventional samples. Organic isn't perfect but drops the count sharply.
4

Store

The single rule that changes everything: never refrigerate a whole tomato. Cold below 12 °C shatters the flavor cell membranes. Once mealy, they don't come back.

Counter
3 – 5 days
Stem-down, out of direct sun, single layer (bruises spread by contact).
Fridge
Only if cut
Cut side down on a plate, sealed. 2 days max. Bring to room temp before eating.
Ripen faster
Paper bag
Traps the tomato's own ethylene. Add a banana or apple to speed further.
Preserve
6 ways
Sun-dried, oven-roasted, passata, paste, fermented salsa, frozen whole.
Why the fridge ruins them
Below 12 °C, chilling injury destroys the enzymes that make aroma compounds. The fruit turns mealy, muted, cardboard. The USDA Beltsville study (2016) mapped exactly which genes shut off — they don't turn back on when warmed.
5

Prep & Cook

Raw for freshness, cooked for depth. Heat and fat unlock lycopene — the pigment your body uses for skin and heart protection. A cooked tomato with olive oil delivers roughly 4× the absorbable lycopene of a raw one.

Basic prep

Wash & core

Cool water, gentle rub. Remove stem scar with knife tip — it's bitter.

Peel

Score X on bottom. Blanch 30s. Ice bath. Skin slips off — for silky sauces.

Seed

Cut in half across the equator. Squeeze gently. Only for dishes that would go watery.

What it can become

Cook with fat, not without
Lycopene is fat-soluble. A slow simmer with olive oil breaks cell walls and dissolves the pigment. The Journal of Agricultural & Food Chemistry (2002) measured 3.8× more absorbable lycopene from tomato sauce with oil vs raw tomato juice.
Don't cook in aluminum
Tomato acid strips the aluminum oxide layer and leaches metal into the sauce. Off-flavor at best, health concern at worst. Stainless, enamel, or seasoned cast iron only.

Pairs / Avoid

Every pairing here has a reason — flavor chemistry, nutrient absorption, or a tradition that survived because it works. Everything in the "avoid" list has a specific failure mode, not just a rumor.

Pairs beautifully with

🌿
Basil
Both share eugenol and linalool. The oils intensify each other's grassy note — the caprese is chemistry, not just tradition.
🫒
Olive oil
Dissolves lycopene for the body. Coats acid so it doesn't sting. Sicilian rule: oil on tomato before salt.
🧄
Garlic
Sulfur compounds balance the sweetness. Slow, low heat — never brown, or bitterness ruins the sauce.
🧀
Mozzarella
Fat softens the acid on your tongue. Milk protein binds lycopene, slowing absorption but extending release.
🧅
Onion
Cooked slow, onion sugars caramelize and mellow tomato's edge. The base of nearly every world sauce.
🥚
Eggs
Yolk fat carries lycopene. Acid tightens egg proteins — shakshuka, tomato omelette, huevos rancheros.
🫘
Chickpea
Vitamin C in tomato boosts iron absorption from the chickpea by ~3×. Chana masala isn't an accident.
🌶️
Cumin
Earthy anchor for tomato's bright top note. The reason Mexican and Indian sauces feel familiar.
🧂
Salt (at rest)
Draws out water from cut tomato. 15 min salted → concentrated flavor, better texture, less watery salad.

Avoid or handle carefully

🥒
Cucumber (raw, at length)
Cucumber has ascorbinase — an enzyme that destroys tomato's vitamin C. Fine in a small salad; not for pickles or juice.
🥛
Milk (raw, cold)
Acid curdles cold milk instantly. Cream in a hot sauce is different — the fat is emulsified and heat stabilizes the casein.
🥔
Potato (same meal, heavy)
Both are nightshades. If you have solanine sensitivity or an inflammatory condition, load stacks the same alkaloids twice.
🍋
Extra citrus in a raw dish
Tomato is already 4% acid. More lemon flattens the fruit's own tartness. Save citrus for cooked dishes that lost brightness.
🥫
Aluminum cookware
Acid leaches metal. Off-flavor, off-color, off the table. Use stainless, enamel, or seasoned cast iron.
🥶
Refrigeration (whole)
Below 12 °C aroma enzymes shut off permanently. Flavor doesn't return with warming.

In the body

Cheap on calories, rich on carotenoids. The famous one is lycopene — a red-orange pigment linked to reduced prostate-cancer risk and better skin UV response in repeated meta-analyses.

Per 100 g
18 kcal
3.9 g carb · 1.2 g fiber · 0.9 g protein · 0.2 g fat
Vitamin C
14 mg
~15% RDA. Fades with cooking — raw for C, cooked for lycopene.
Lycopene
3 mg raw · 15 mg paste
Paste and canned deliver 5× the pigment of fresh. Cook with fat to absorb it.
Potassium
237 mg
Meaningful for blood pressure — one cup of sauce ≈ half a banana.
Boosts absorption
Heat + oil (breaks cell walls, dissolves pigment). Vitamin C from the tomato itself boosts iron uptake from legumes eaten together.
Watch out for
Oral allergy syndrome in some birch-pollen sufferers (mouth itch after raw tomato — cooked usually fine). Some people with autoimmune flare-ups track improvement after cutting all nightshades for 30 days.
